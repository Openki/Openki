# FAQ

## General

### What is Openki?

Openki is an open education platform where groups can be proposed, discussed,
developed, organized, and published. The focus is on promoting
self-organization, bringing people and initiatives together, and ensuring a
barrier-free exchange of experience and knowledge. The groups are accessible to
all, regardless of their financial situation. The organization behind Openki is
non-profit and the software is open source.

Find out more about [Openki and its philosophy](https://about.openki.net/) or
read about [the basic concepts behind the Openki
platform](https://about.openki.net/?page_id=1216).

### What does the name "Openki" actually mean?

Openki is a Ukrainian word (опеньки) referring to a type of fungus commonly
known as the honey fungus or [_Armillaria
mellea_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillaria_mellea). The name refers
specifically to the [mycelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycelium), the part
of a fungus that is usually not visible when we think of a “mushroom.” It
consists of thousands of thin threads forming a dense underground network, which
in the case of Openki can span several square kilometers and weigh hundreds of
tons. Many plants are connected through these mycelial networks, relying on them
for germination, nutrient exchange, and even communication. Above the surface,
the mushrooms—the "fruiting bodies"—pop up unexpectedly and unpredictably.

Openki (the fungus) feeds on both living shrubs and dead organic material.
Philosophically, the mycelium serves as a metaphor for a transformation to an
alternative model of education: it composts the hierarchical branch-and-root
structure of the "ancient Greek tree of knowledge"—where disciplines are
separated and subordinated—and connects them into a horizontal interconnected
network of knowledge.

### Who owns Openki?

Openki belongs to you, to me, and to everyone who participates. The application
is open source and freely available under the [GNU AGPL
license](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.de.html). Openki is maintained by
the collectively organized non-profit association "KOPF," which has been
developing the platform over the past years.

### Is Openki a social network?

Yes and no. We provide a tool for self-organization, and social interaction is
naturally part of that. However, unlike typical social media platforms, we want
you to spend as little time as possible on our website and instead use that time
to organize real-world learning experiences with great people.

### I have an idea for a technical improvement. Can I contribute?

That’s great! We need your feedback to make Openki even better. We have dozens
of ideas we want to work on, but we currently limit ourselves to the most
essential features to keep Openki easy to use and maintainable within our
capacity. Nevertheless, we are always happy to receive feedback or feature
proposals that could further simplify self-organization.

Both the software and its development process are fully transparent. You can
view or join discussions on our next technical steps in our [issue
tracker](https://gitlab.com/Openki/Openki/issues). Alternatively, you can send
your suggestions via email. Also, please upvote issues and features you care
about; it helps us prioritize our roadmap and decide what to work on next.

You can also help translate Openki using our [online translation
tool](https://gitlab.com/Openki/Openki/-/wikis/translation).

### Who administers the Openki.net website?

Administration and technical maintenance (addressing user issues, cleanup, bug
fixing, and improvements) are handled by a volunteer-based collective. Our goal
is to establish similar local admin collectives for each region where Openki is
active.

## Die Rollen auf Openki

### What is an interested User?

A User is "interested" when they join a group. Beyond joining events and staying
informed, interested can also take on the roles of Mentor, Host, or join the
coordination team.

### What is a Mentor?

Mentors have a certain level of knowledge and/or experience in a topic and guide
participants through the learning process. They are usually responsible for
preparation and follow-up.\
Unlike teachers, mentors are typically not formally trained for this role but
contribute based on their experience and knowledge.

### What is a (Coordination Team)?

The Coordinators are responsible for coordinating inside a group (creating
events, editing descriptions, making polls). The person who proposes a group
automatically becomes the first member of the Coordination-Team and can add
others.

### What is a host?

A host is responsible for the venue where a group takes place. This includes
access to the space, coordinating availability, acting as a contact person, and
ensuring that house rules are communicated and respected.

### What is an organization?

Organizations are existing communities or associations that use Openki to
organize and publish their groups. They can just promote groups and they can
also help organizing them when they are granted coordination rights.

## Groups

### How do I organize my first Openki group meeting?

You can find detailed tips and instructions in this [PDF
guide](https://about.openki.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/How-to-organize-my-first-Openki-course.pdf).

### What is an Openki group?

An Openki group is more than just a traditional classroom setting. It involves
discussions regarding content and goals, finding mentors, and organizing events.
Like a ship, all participants can help steer the group and influence its
direction. Formats can range from lectures and workshops to learning groups,
reading circles, or alternative education projects.

A group typically begins as a "suggestion" — an idea, a wish, or a question.
Others can join, mentors may emerge, and a venue may be found. Once an event is
scheduled, the group “takes place.” Groups can be one-time events or recurring
series.

### What groups can be organized?

Any topic is welcome: from cooking to nuclear physics, modern dance to
literature. Groups are categorized (science, sports, music, etc.).
Discriminatory content (e.g. sexist or racist) is strictly excluded.

### Can groups cost something?

Yes, but prices are considered suggested contributions (donations). Participants
may pay more or less. Only actual costs (materials, space, travel, etc.) may be
fixed.

### I want to host a group but have no space...

Depending on the region, Openki has a list of available venues. All users can
also list their own spaces—from factories and living rooms to gardens—to be used
as venues. To use a space, contact the person listed as the host or someone who
has previously organized an event at that location.

### When does a group take place?

As soon as someone proposes a group, it is published as a "suggestion" and
appears with a dashed border in the group overview.\
For a group to appear in the calendar, a member of the Coordination-Team must
create an "Event". This is done after the date, time, and location have been
defined.

### How binding is the registration for a group?

The level of commitment required depends on the specific group and should be
stated in the group description.

### Where do groups take place?

Everywhere! The group is responsible for finding a suitable venue and a
[Host](#what-is-a-host). Users are encouraged to share their own spaces. We are
constantly working on expanding the list of available venues in every region.

### Can I archive group materials?

Currently, there is no dedicated system for archiving files. However,
discussions and links remain visible in the group description, and past groups
stay accessible.

### In what language are the groups held? Why is the group description not translated?

Groups are typically held in the language in which they are published.
Multilingual groups will be listed as such in their descriptions.

### Why are past groups still visible?

The idea is that a topic is never truly "finished." An idea may remain relevant,
and users can reactivate a group or access its documentation at any time. Active
groups with upcoming events can be found in the
[Calendar](https://openki.net/calendar).

### I need a specific tool to organize my group!

Many free tools are available online and can be linked directly in your group
description. For example:

- Create a wiki:
  [https://www.wikispaces.com/content/classroom](https://www.wikispaces.com/content/classroom)
- Collaborative writing: [Etherpad](https://etherpad.wikimedia.org) or
  [Hackpad](https://hackpad.com)
- Simple voting/scheduling: [Dudle](https://dudle.inf.tu-dresden.de/) or
  [Framadate](https://framadate.org)
- Group decision making: [Loomio](https://loomio.org)
- File sharing: [WeTransfer](https://wetransfer.com/) or a self-hosted
  [NextCloud](https://nextcloud.com) instance.

## Mentoring

### What is a mentor?

See ["What is a Mentor?"](#what-is-a-mentor)

### Who can be a mentor?

Any user can sign up to be a mentor for a group.

### Does a group always need a mentor?

No. Some groups function perfectly well with all participants sharing knowledge
equally without a designated mentor.

### How do I know if a Mentor is reliable?

If you are unsure, contact the person in advance and use your best judgment.

### Why can't I charge a fixed fee as a mentor?

Openki aims to make education accessible to everyone, regardless of financial
situation.

## Organizations

### What are organizations?

Organizations can be any entity that uses Openki to help their community to
collaboratively organize knowledge exchange or manage and promote their
educational offerings.

### How can we register our organization?

Any user can create an organization profile via their account settings:
[https://openki.net/organization/create](https://openki.net/organization/create)

### Can we show Openki groups on our own website?

Yes! Embedding options (iframes links) are available in the organization
settings.. More information is available in the [Openki
wiki](https://gitlab.com/Openki/Openki/wikis/Frames).

## Safety

### How is abusive behavior prevented?

Identifying and reporting discriminatory harmful content is the responsibility
of all users. The administration handles moderation and ensures transparency
regarding any content that is removed.

### How do I know if I'm safe attending a group?

Contact the organizer in advance if you have concerns. Use common sense and, if
you feel uneasy, avoid attending alone.

### How does Openki protect user privacy?

Openki utilizes modern encryption and offers personalized privacy settings. We
do not sell user data, we do not track your behavior, and we do not allow third
parties to track your interests on our platform.

### How secure is the Openki website?

Openki.net uses SSL/TLS encryption by default—the same technology used for
online banking. However, your participation in groups is visible to others, so
using a pseudonym is possible.

## Regions

### What is an Openki Region?

Every group, event and venue belongs to a geographic region, such as a city or
village. You can choose to browse groups in a specific region or view all
regions simultaneously.

### How can I create a new Openki Region?

Our long-term vision is for Openki to be a global platform where people can add
their own regions or even self-host the software that connects with other Openki
instances.\
Currently, we add new regions for Openki.net upon request from individuals who
demonstrate a commitment to activating a local community and ensuring it remains
free of spam and aligned with Openki's values.

We have an e-mail list for
[region-admins](https://lists.lists.openki.net/mailman/listinfo/region-admins)
to exchange ideas and solve common issues. We are also developing specific
"region admin" tools to allow for local moderation and management.

### How can I contact my region's administrator?

Currently, region admins cannot be contacted directly. Please send an inquiry to
region-admin-owner@lists.openki.net and we will forward your message.
